> "I was a victim of a series of accidents. As are we all." -- Vonnegut. 

You probably could tell from my last post, I've been reading another book by Kurt Vonnegut. This time, out of a happy accident while perusing the library near me. 

my knowledge of this one was sparse, besides it being one of his earlier works. The story mainly follows a fellow named Malachi Constant. Besides his cool name, and mutual curiosity about these sirens on titan, I wasn't too interested in the protagonist this time around. He was a bland rich man without much charisma to him. luckily, the story that propels him around the solar system is mysterious and fantastical. It's another Kurt Vonnegut classic mixing humor, space, time, aliens, and dogs. The main theme of the book is free will. something so large in scope, and deep in its understanding that people could talk about it for a life time. That's exactly where the book takes us. The life of Malachi. Not just him, but the lifecycle of humanity itself. 

## Hypnotic Anarchy
either you understand at once what it is, or there is no sense trying to explain it to you. The book opens on a quote from Malachi where he describes his good luck in life. "Somebody up there likes me". Through his interactions with Winston Niles Rumfoord,  we start to think holy shit this guy isn't lucky at all. He is stripped of his memory, his fortune, his world, his best friend, and his sense of self. He's trapped with his worst enemy on a god forsaken rock for years on end with nothing to do but look around for a way out. The titular Sirens of Titan that initially entice Malachi and the reader amount to a rude awakening near the end of the book. In his final moments, he is told this phrase again. Oddly enough, he believes it more than ever. Does he understand why? Does he even care why? I can tell you this, i sure don't care why. I'm just happy he's happy.
